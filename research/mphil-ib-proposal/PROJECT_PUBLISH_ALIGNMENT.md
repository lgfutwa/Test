## 1. Session 1 readiness pack (use tomorrow)

### One-sentence point of difference

> Unlike studies that treat FinTech as an adoption or financing enabler, this study specifies **FinTech Resource Orchestration (FTRO)** as the firm-level mechanism through which African technology-enabled service SMEs convert digital financial resources into INV-informed cross-border capabilities.

### Elevator pitch (≈45 seconds)

African SMEs now have more FinTech tools than ever, yet many still struggle to internationalise. Existing research shows that FinTech and digitalisation are associated with better financing or expansion outcomes, but it rarely explains *how* resource-constrained firms turn heterogeneous digital financial tools into the capabilities early internationalisers need. Grounded in International New Venture theory, and importing Resource Orchestration Theory as mechanism machinery, this study develops the FinTech-Orchestrated Internationalisation (FOI) Framework. Using an embedded multiple-case design across Kenya, Nigeria and South Africa, it reconstructs how SMEs structure, bundle and leverage FinTech under organisational and institutional conditions, with providers and regulators as embedded informants.

### Burning platform (manager / practitioner)

Cross-border payments frictions, liquidity gaps, compliance costs and institutional voids still block African service SMEs from converting digital opportunity into durable foreign-market presence. Managers need a process model for selecting, integrating and redeploying FinTech configurations—not another adoption checklist.

### Academic difference (why cite you)

The study answers INV’s open provenance/formation problem for independent ventures: cross-border capabilities need not be endowment or incumbent inheritance; they can be assembled by orchestrating externally provided, regulatorily gated digital financial resources under specifiable conditions.

### Three-sentence state of play

1. INV / born-global research explains early internationalisation through unique resources, alternative governance, networks and organisational capabilities, but leaves open how independent ventures without incumbent sponsorship assemble that resource base (Oviatt & McDougall, 1994; Zahra, 2005; Rumyantseva & Welch, 2023).
2. FinTech and digital-IB studies link digital financial and platform resources to financing access and internationalisation outcomes, yet typically treat technology as antecedent rather than specifying firm-level conversion mechanisms (Lee & Shin, 2018; Jean & Kim, 2020; Anwar et al., 2023).
3. Resource Orchestration Theory supplies the missing managerial process vocabulary—structuring, bundling, leveraging—but has not been systematically applied to FinTech-enabled SME internationalisation in African emerging markets (Sirmon et al., 2007, 2011).

### Research aim

To explain how technology-enabled service SMEs in Kenya, Nigeria and South Africa orchestrate FinTech resources to generate cross-border capabilities that shape internationalisation outcomes, and under which organisational and institutional conditions this process strengthens or fails.
